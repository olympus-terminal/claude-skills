# Bio-Noir Scenery

Create varied scenes that belong to one coherent world: advanced architecture and a thriving tropical ecosystem designed to function together. Preserve the useful bio-noir lineage through concrete choices—deep nocturnal contrast, refined futurism, integrated bioluminescent ecology, crisp materials, and controlled accent light. Do not put film, franchise, or city names into production prompts by default; their associated tropes can leak into otherwise unrelated scenes.

## Defaults

- Compose natively for an extreme horizontal panorama. End each prompt with `--ar 32:9 --s 50` unless the user overrides either setting.
- Do not add version, Raw, Chaos, quality, or negative parameters unless explicitly requested.
- When producing a set, give each scene a short title and one copy-ready prompt. Keep the prose compact enough to preserve a clear visual hierarchy.
- If the user asks to save the prompts, write a timestamped `.txt` file without overwriting an existing collection.

## Design Language

- Vary the scene rather than repeating one megacity composition. Suitable scales range from intimate interiors and pedestrian streets to transit, civic landscapes, temples, observatories, rivers, and monumental city views.
- Do not promote a real-world place, architectural fashion, vehicle type, or earlier one-off scene into the default design language. Keep each temporary influence local to the set that requested it unless the user explicitly makes it persistent.
- Depict superior, working design. Structures are intact, purposeful, navigable, and intelligently maintained. Make circulation, drainage, lighting, transit, access, and planted areas feel deliberately integrated.
- Make smart futurism unmistakable through decisive structural mass, enclosed transit and technical interfaces, elegant weather protection, automated systems, advanced materials, and—when the scene calls for it—megacity, orbital-station, or tower scale. Do not substitute rustic boardwalks, exposed timber decks, small utility sheds, improvised bridges, or eco-resort styling for advanced design.
- Do not rely on visible people to prove that a place functions. Imply active use through operating transit, calibrated systems, illuminated rooms, orderly furnishings, maintained paths, stocked facilities, and other environmental evidence. Omit human figures unless the user explicitly requests them.
- Treat jungle growth as designed ecology, not neglect. Place roots, moss, ferns, and trees in credible courtyards, terraces, channels, roofs, conservatories, or protected root zones.
- Ancient architecture may have patina, moss, softened stone edges, or other evidence of age, but its structural logic and present function remain coherent. Weathering is material history, not catastrophe.
- Pair atmospheric elements with hard material anchors: mist with concrete, foliage with glass or stone, and water with clearly bounded channels or reflecting pools.

## Composition and Lighting

- Exploit the full 32:9 width with lateral movement, separated foreground/midground/background, and one unmistakable focal hierarchy. Do not merely stretch a centered conventional scene.
- Use one dominant directional light source and at most one restrained accent color. Preserve deep shadow masses while keeping important architecture and near-field foliage legible.
- Confine fog and atmospheric softness to a named spatial layer such as the distant skyline, a lower valley, or the space beneath a platform.
- Do not use active rain, storms, wet pavement, water droplets, or rain-slick reflections as generic bio-noir atmosphere. Occupied, transit, retail, research, and technical environments remain dry, weather-protected, and visually clear. Use rain only when the user explicitly requests it, and keep it outside protected human and technical zones.
- Prefer crisp silhouettes, resolved leaf edges, clean reflections, and legible concrete, glass, metal, stone, ceramic, and advanced-composite surfaces.

## Structural Legibility

- Bound delicate infrastructure by count, placement, and support. Prefer "two broad catwalks on thick concrete piers" or "three antennas mounted separately along the roofline" over vague plural networks.
- Give bridges, platforms, roofs, antennas, and cantilevers visible attachment points and sufficient structural mass. Preserve clear negative space between thin elements.
- Avoid tangled cables, intricate scaffolding, forests of antennas, networks of catwalks, or other unbounded linework that can become black cobwebs.

## Exclude from the Concept

- Do not default to abandoned, flooded, collapsed, shattered, derelict, improvised, debris-filled, or blackout environments.
- Do not use structural damage as a shortcut for atmosphere. Water belongs in designed waterways or exterior weather systems unless the user specifically requests an adverse event.
- Avoid visible human subjects by default. If the user explicitly requests people, keep their number, placement, scale, and activity precise and subordinate to the environment.
- Avoid softness triggers such as `painterly`, `oil painting`, `watercolor`, `impasto`, `dreamy`, `ethereal`, `soft-focus`, `smeared`, `drippy`, or `lush painterly detail`.
- Avoid making every object glow. Use emissive moss, displays, signage, or vehicle lights sparingly and hierarchically.

## Final Check

Before delivering each prompt, confirm that:

1. The scene reads as a good day in a capable, functioning environment.
2. The composition was conceived for 32:9 and has one clear focal order.
3. Every thin elevated element is countable, located, separated, and visibly supported.
4. Age or weathering does not imply failed engineering.
5. Lighting has one primary direction, restrained accents, deep contrast, and crisp key surfaces.
6. Atmospheric softness is spatially contained.
7. No active rain or wet-weather trope appears around occupied or technical space unless explicitly requested and visibly managed.
8. No human figure appears unless explicitly requested.
9. Futuristic infrastructure reads as substantial, enclosed or protected where appropriate, and deliberately engineered rather than rustic or improvised.
10. The prompt ends with exactly the requested minimal parameter suffix.
